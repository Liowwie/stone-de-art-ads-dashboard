Quarter Summary — Q2 2026 vs Q1 2026

Overall performance

Q2 2026 generated +63.4% more Actual Paid Leads than Q1 2026 (153 → 250) on +55.9% more spend (RM 10,491 → RM 16,356). Lead growth outpacing budget growth is why Actual Paid CPL improved rather than worsened despite the bigger budget.

Overall efficiency

CTR improved from 6.44% to 6.91%, and Actual Paid CPL moved from RM 69 to RM 65 (-4.6%). Cost per Google Ads Conversion, by contrast, moved from RM 309 to RM 303 — a reminder that the platform metric and the CSV-verified metric can move in different directions, which is exactly why Actual Paid CPL is treated as the primary efficiency number in this report.

Q2 2026 is a genuine efficiency improvement on real, verified lead data — more paid leads at a lower cost per lead, with better CTR alongside it. CTA / Recommended Actions

What the data tells us

Ads are generating more real leads at a lower cost per lead (153 → 250 Actual Paid Leads, CPL -4.6%), but that growth has no confirmed sale behind it yet, and WhatsApp is carrying nearly all of the demand.

What we should protect

The current Collection / Signature Works budget split and the improving WhatsApp source-tagging discipline — both are measurably working and don't need disruption.

What needs fixing
  • Sales-attribution tracking — request an updated sales log covering Jan–Jul 2026 with Paid/Organic/Unsure tags
  • Showroom-visit logging — confirm whether every walk-in, including from website leads, is being recorded
  • WhatsApp lead qualification — reduce the 129 Irrelevant / 94 Just-said-hi share with a preset qualifying question flow
Recommended focus

Next month: audit the 134 Unsure-tagged Q2 leads for recoverable source data; verify Walk-In logging with the showroom team.

Next quarter: hold total spend near the current level until sales attribution is closed; if adding budget, weight it toward Collection (lower cost per conversion); keep the keyword focus on high-intent terms (natural stone, marble/granite countertops) that already convert.
